# Sponsor brief and pilot worksheet — draft

SignalKey is an independent Magnexis concept: press a physical button to run a local developer workflow, then see its observed completion state. Intended initial users are developers who repeatedly run builds/tests and want an ambient result without another account or cloud dependency.

Evidence so far: local software simulator runs a real passing/failing sample, records actual exit output, supports cancellation and authenticated SDK status. Tests and screenshot are in `docs/evidence`. There is no working physical board, customer validation, paid pilot, partner, reservation or sales evidence.

Specific proposed in-kind request: assemble and electrically review **one to three Pico H / switch / level-shifted RGB-ring prototypes**, including safe current-limited power and a soldered harness, or provide an equivalent engineering review/printed carrier. Owner lacks electronics/soldering equipment. Agree scope, attribution, deliverables, ownership and any obligations before accepting support. A sponsor contributes agreed resources; a contract manufacturer supplies paid services. Neither implies a partnership until agreed.

Milestones: software pass/fail evidence → reviewed prototype and measured current/latency → five-to-ten engineering boards → twenty-to-fifty pilot/validation units → production-process validation. Counts are proposals, not commitments.

Interview prompts: Which command do you repeat most? What happens while it runs? How do you currently notice completion or failure? When would a desk light be useful versus distracting? What would you bind to one button? What would make enabling a command feel safe? Which operating systems/hubs/cables do you use? What competing tools already solve this? What would justify keeping this on your desk?

Pilot form fields: participant consent and anonymous ID; host OS/build; device serial/revisions; task/config; expected/observed result; missed/duplicate press count; false or stale indication; setup minutes; clarity without relying on color; button feel; cable stability; errors/reproduction; free-text usefulness; permission to follow up. Do not infer purchase intent from politeness. Keep contact details separate from technical logs and set a retention period before recruiting.
